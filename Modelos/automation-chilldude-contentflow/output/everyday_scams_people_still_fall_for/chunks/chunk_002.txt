You're scrolling, minding your own business, and then BAM! An ad for a revolutionary new product. Maybe it’s a weight loss supplement that promises you’ll look like a Greek god in a week, or a skincare cream that swears it’ll erase 20 years from your face faster than you can say "botox." The catch? There isn't one, they say! Just pay shipping for a "free trial." Sounds harmless, right? You punch in your credit card details, thinking, "What's the worst that could happen?" Well, the worst, my friend, is that you've just signed up for a recurring subscription that costs more than your monthly rent, and canceling it is harder than getting a straight answer from a politician. These companies are masters of fine print and dark patterns. They hide the auto-renewal clause in a font so small it needs a microscope, or bury it under layers of click-through agreements. Before you know it, you're getting charged $99.99 every month for a product you tried once, hated, and then promptly forgot about. It's the digital equivalent of that gym membership you signed up for in January and completely abandoned by February, except this one keeps bleeding your bank account dry without you even having to feel guilty about not working out.
