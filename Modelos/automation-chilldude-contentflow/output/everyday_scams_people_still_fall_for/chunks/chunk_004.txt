Ring, ring! Your phone buzzes. You answer, and a stern-sounding voice with an exotic accent informs you that they're from "Microsoft" or "Apple" and your computer is riddled with viruses, or perhaps it's sending out spam emails faster than a politician sends out fundraising requests. They tell you your digital life is in peril, and only they can save it. All you need to do is grant them remote access to your computer, and maybe, just maybe, buy some extremely overpriced "protection software" or pay a "diagnostic fee" with gift cards. Now, a rational person might think, "Wait, why would Microsoft call *me*? And how do they even know I have a problem?" But in that moment of panic, when your digital photos and bank accounts feel threatened, logic often takes a vacation. People, these companies do not cold-call you about your computer problems. It's like your car dealership calling to say your tires are flat without you having told them anything about your car – it just doesn't happen. They'll install actual malware, steal your personal data, or simply charge you an exorbitant amount for fixing a problem that never existed. It’s a classic, but it still reels in victims like fish on a Friday night.
