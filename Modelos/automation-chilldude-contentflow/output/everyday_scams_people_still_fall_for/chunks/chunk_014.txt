Your phone rings, or you get a text message, and it's from someone claiming to be from your gas company, electric company, or even the IRS. The message is always urgent and terrifying: "Your power will be shut off in 30 minutes if you don't pay your overdue bill immediately!" or "You owe back taxes and a warrant has been issued for your arrest!" They demand payment right away, often insisting on gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency, because, you know, that's how legitimate government agencies and utility companies collect payments. Yeah, no. These scams rely on fear and urgency to bypass your common sense. No legitimate utility company will threaten immediate shut-off without multiple prior warnings, and they certainly won't demand payment via gift cards. The IRS will never call you out of the blue threatening arrest. They do everything by mail, and they're notoriously slow, not lightning-fast with threats. If you get one of these calls, hang up. If you're genuinely concerned, call the utility company or the IRS directly using the number on their official website or your bill, not the number the scammer gives you. Your money and your freedom are not decided by a prepaid Visa.
